## Plot

Im Si-wan posts a short terror threat online in English, picks a plane at random, boards it and carries out an attack.

On the ground, the detective Song Kang-ho, who has seen the video, runs himself ragged trying to find the culprit and a solution.

So it's a film that puts the terror attack in the middle and shows both the inside of the plane in the air and the people on the ground.

## Plot with spoilers

Lee Byung-hun's daughter goes to the men's toilet, presumably because the queue for the women's was long and she was desperate. She has short hair. And right then she makes eye contact with Im Si-wan, who is hiding a canister for the attack under his arm.

Im Si-wan follows the child, confirms they're going to Hawaii, and boards that flight.

Song Kang-ho's wife is on it too.

Song Kang-ho, a detective, sees the threat video and, anxious because his own wife is on a plane, somehow tracks down Im Si-wan's house, where a body lets him start the investigation quickly. He also finds a video there of Im Si-wan modifying a lethal virus and testing it on rats.

He smuggles the powdered virus under his arm, takes it out in the toilet, sprays it using an asthma inhaler, and people are infected. It's a virus that ruptures capillaries. It's said to be extremely contagious. The man who used the toilet first dies, then the flight attendant who checked on him, and then Im Si-wan. He dies vomiting blood.

Then someone leaks it, and the whole world learns there's a virus aboard that plane.

People need treatment fast, but they can't land in Hawaii or in Japan — until, thankfully, the pharmaceutical company Im Si-wan used to work for says it has a vaccine, so they head for Korea. But there's opposition in Korea too, since nobody knows whether it works on the modified virus, so the passengers all decide together not to land.

Then Song Kang-ho personally threatens people at gunpoint, takes the virus and then the antiviral, sees that it works, and tells them to come back. There had been protesters demonstrating against them coming, but on hearing the plane wouldn't land they fall silent, and once they hear it worked on Song Kang-ho they withdraw entirely. So the plane lands safely and everyone survives without side effects, apart from those who died on board.

Lee Byung-hun flies it from Japan to Korea. He'd been a captain before, lost two flight attendants in an accident (one of them another captain's wife) and quit flying — and with the captain and first officer both collapsed, he flies and lands it.

At the end the passengers have a barbecue party, and it closes on Song Kang-ho and Lee Byung-hun exchanging a look.

## Afterwards

I saw the trailer when I went to IMAX for Alienoid and it looked so good.

Im Si-wan going "I just want them all~ to die!!" made me think oh, nice acting~~, and I was curious what kind of attack a Korean film could stage inside an aeroplane, and what they'd do with that cast.

And I did genuinely enjoy it, but how do I put this..

Well made and badly made?

At the start, over a black screen in white text, it explains the emergency declaration — "declared when in danger", "has the same effect as martial law", "emergency declaration" — and puts up the title. I'd just been talking with a friend that day about going to Vietnam, so for a second I thought

"wait, we're going to Vietnam, should I even be watching this...?" But Vietnam should be fine. For a start the attack itself didn't feel likely..

Early on Im Si-wan turns up asking "which plane carries the most people?" and I was watching thinking he's handsome.. and then he suddenly starts being obnoxious to a flight attendant. While carrying out the attack he seems fully deranged so I didn't think much of it, but seeing him harass someone at work irritated me enough that I could spend the whole film wishing he'd just go home.

I think I enjoyed it a lot up to around the halfway point. It said at the start that it screened at Cannes? or won there?, so I was watching thinking wow, no wonder it went to Cannes..

Maybe it's because I don't watch many blockbusters or disaster films, but the camera framing felt new to me.

The framing shot close from behind the driver's seat when the plane takes off, or when Song Kang-ho is chasing someone in a car, and the spinning fall when the captain is infected and collapses — I felt like I was on board and falling with them. I was completely absorbed.

And when comments pile up on news articles telling them not to land in Korea, and people protest, I imagined people I love being on that plane and got so invested that it hurt and I teared up a little. I know it's dangerous, of course, but does that mean all those people should just fall into the sea ㅜㅠㅠ

The first infected person deteriorates so fast — nosebleed, eyes rupturing, spitting out what looks like a litre of blood — that I was watching very tense, wondering how anyone survives a virus that quick.

The parts I didn't like:

the reversals / the melodrama / Song Kang-ho and the transport minister and the pharmaceutical company

## The reversals

There is of course the premise that a poorly understood virus is on board and it's dangerous, but

1. Virus breaks out en route to Hawaii → told not to land in the US

2. Try to make an emergency landing in Japan → told not to land → go anyway → the Self-Defense Forces are sent to fire warning shots and they leave

(if they were going to leave anyway, why go to Japan and get threatened by the SDF)

3. Heading for Korea, the passengers apologise and decide not to land → Lee Byung-hun cuts all communication

4. The antiviral is confirmed to work but comms are down, so families contact a passenger → they turn back

That felt.. odd. Honestly I'm not sure why the film is called Emergency Declaration. The first officer does solemnly say **"declaring an emergency"** on the way to Japan, but even then they can't land anywhere and in the end they'd all have died if not for Song Kang-ho.. I don't know why the declaration was made.

## The melodrama

The article comments made me sad, but the melodrama that follows broke the spell. There were smoother bits along the way and those were watchable. But after the passengers go "let's.. not land....ㅜㅜ" they start making video calls to their families, and honestly only Song Kang-ho's wife's part was needed. Showing seven or eight passengers on video calls from the phone-screen point of view wasn't sad at all, it just made me think, here we go again. And on top of that, a little later the drug works on Song Kang-ho, the news breaks, they get the call, and the passengers go "the antiviral.. works!!!" and happily head home... I think the video-call scene could simply have been cut. Actually the whole "we won't land" decision could have gone too. If they'd ignored the protests and landed right then they'd have had fuel left and landed safely.

And how did people gather at the airport to protest in under half a day, and then disperse instantly at one line about the antiviral working;;

## Song Kang-ho and the transport minister and the pharmaceutical company

Im Si-wan was apparently fired from the pharmaceutical company for testing a virus on people. So the company has a vaccine, and Song Kang-ho throws himself at them demanding they hand over everything — and then the transport minister walks up behind him going "I'm the Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport. Drag this out and it won't end with your company folding", walks straight in and gets the vaccine in one move. Of course Song Kang-ho's efforts produced a whistleblower, which is how they knew where the vaccine was, but still.. the pharmaceutical company and the minister felt like characters made of stock images. Honestly Song Kang-ho might be too. Although in his case his wife was on the plane...

Song Kang-ho threatening people at gunpoint and taking the virus is impressive but also made me go, what?

And Im Si-wan picking up a virus abroad and testing it on his own team members also made me go, what?

I was absorbed and genuinely enjoyed it and thought it was well made. But the more I thought about it afterwards, the more I felt it could have been better, so 2.8 stars... From past the midpoint it started to feel messy. The SDF firing and the video-call relay were too much.

While watching I was fixated on the article comments and the protests and felt so sad, but now that it's over.. what stays with me are the disappointments. The take-off, the crash sequence and the car scenes were good.
