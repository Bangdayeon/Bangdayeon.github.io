## Plot

Sang-soon, a rising candidate for parliament, is a very good liar. Her grandmother had a great deal of money, and Sang-soon set up a foundation to donate all of it, which people love her for. But when the grandmother left a note about returning to nature and disappeared, people misunderstood and assumed she had died — and Sang-soon never corrects them. In reality the grandmother genuinely did go off to live in nature.

So the grandmother, living in hiding for the sake of Sang-soon's votes, wishes that Sang-soon might live without lying — and the wish comes true. Sang-soon has to run her campaign unable to lie.

## Afterwards

Fun! There's a bit of melodrama here and there, but I think it's cut off at reasonably appropriate points.

Sang-soon has been stained by politics, but her nature is good. The actual administrator of the science school she backed embezzled 150 billion won, and when a reporter breaks it, Sang-soon regrets it. Why did she never think to look into it herself, why did she not suspect anything. She knows, of course, that it was the easy road. So she hands the reporter a USB stick believing it holds a video of her messing about at karaoke. To blow herself up. (I'm not sure why that counts as blowing herself up.) But the USB turned out to hold footage of all sorts of politicians committing corruption, buying sex, abusing their power — so Sang-soon simply opened a scandal. The Joo Sang-soon gate..!

Everyone learns that the 150 billion won corruption wasn't Sang-soon's doing, but she resigns anyway. After that she lives somewhere ordinary, wears her shoes until they're worn out, and even though she can lie again, the film ends with her running as a candidate for mayor of Seoul, still speaking her mind bluntly as the mouth of truth.

Light and fun to watch. It made me wish people like that actually existed.
