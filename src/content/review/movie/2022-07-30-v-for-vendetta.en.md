Spoilers ahead

## Plot

It's set in the Britain of 2040, where a dictatorship took power amid the chaos after the Third World War and an outbreak of a highly lethal virus. Bugging homes, curfews, broadcast censorship and the death penalty are all established, and the leader is even called Sutler.

The heroine Evey (Natalie Portman) is out somewhere after curfew, gets caught by police-like men and is about to be assaulted, when a masked V appears, saves her, and shows her the courthouse being blown up.

After that V takes over a broadcast station and gives a speech to wake up the people being ruled, telling them he will blow up Parliament on 5 November a year from now and that they should gather in front of it. Evey helps V escape at that moment, and that is where their connection begins.

## Afterwards

After that, V brings Evey to his home, she escapes, he recaptures her, shaves her head, tortures her, and keeps threatening execution if she will not comply — and when she tells him to kill her he says she has courage, and that the whole pretend imprisonment was a device to wake her up;; The absolute lunatic...

And Evey gets a bit angry and then accepts that he was right. On 5 November they do succeed in blowing up Parliament. V dies. He straps steel plates to his body, fights gunmen with four knives, beats all of them single-handedly, comes back and dies.

Evey loads V onto the bomb train he had prepared and sends it off, blowing up Parliament, and people come wearing the masks V had handed out while alive and watch it explode.

I had heard so often that V for Vendetta is a masterpiece, and I knew it was about revolution and freedom, so I had been meaning to watch it — and honestly I am not sure. It is true that it has a lot of famous lines. But somehow... a lot of it felt off.

First, what V does felt less like a revolution than a revenge story. V is a victim of human experimentation carried out by the government. When everyone else was dying of the virus, V alone mutated, gained superhuman speed and strength, and escaped. Through those experiments the government obtained a powerful virus, released it in schools, the subway and the sewers to manufacture fear, and used that to secure its position as a dictatorship.

Writing it out, it really is talking about freedom.. but strip away the things V says officially and it feels like a revenge story. As if V wanted revenge and was conditioned into talking about freedom..? V kills the people involved in the experiments, kills Sutler by way of the second-in-command, then kills the second-in-command himself. Maybe it is because of the experiment thread...

Next, I cannot quite understand people ending up under a dictatorship again after a Third World War that followed a Second World War caused by dictatorship. The police roaming around catching people during curfew sexually harass Evey on the spot while fiddling with their flies;; There is a curfew, there is broadcast censorship, and the police do not even look authoritative — they are straight-up thugs. And yet nobody thought about how the government got this bad until V told them that the citizens' looking away played a part?

And in the end V does the entire revolution alone. He kills everyone involved in the experiments by himself, approaches the second-in-command and has him bring Sutler in, kills Sutler, then kills the second-in-command's henchmen and the man himself. Because the power he got from the experiments lets him move insanely fast, he can kill eight to ten armed men in an instant.

The citizens spend a year spray-painting V symbols on walls and then watch the Parliament firework show V prepared on 5 November.

Of course it takes courage for powerless people to do anything. And there is a scene where a child is shot dead on the spot while painting a V. I do know it is never easy — but in a film shouting about freedom, having one superpowered man named V wake everyone up and kill the government's key figures and blow up the courthouse and Parliament does not land for me.

You could of course read V as standing for the people. The scene where everyone watches Parliament explode wearing V's Guy Fawkes mask, and Evey's line that V was her father and her mother and her brother and you and me — all of us — support that.

But within the film V does carry trauma from his past, does hold a desire for revenge against the government, and does show a soft spot because of Evey. His face and his name are never revealed, and yet his human, personal side keeps surfacing, so he did not read to me as a stand-in for the people carrying out a revolution.

So personally I am not sure.

And shaving Evey's head, locking her in a place dressed up like a prison, barely feeding her, waterboarding her, and then, when she gets angry, saying "thanks to me you got to confirm your courage" is infuriating. Someone producing good results is not automatically someone you want to be close to..
