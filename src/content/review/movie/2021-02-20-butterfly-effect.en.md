Finally watched The Butterfly Effect! It's from 2004, so I was five. I can't quite remember how many time-travel films I've seen... About Time, The Time Traveler's Wife, Your Name, Interstellar, Before I Fall, Edge of Tomorrow — around that many, I think.

## What I felt

It's heavier and darker than other time-travel films. He does seem to make the best available choice by the end, but even that isn't exactly refreshing, and getting there was brutal. He earns it by living through an enormous number of worst-case endings.

Honestly there were plenty of moments where I thought "wait, why is he trying to fix it *like that*?" — but he has a good memory, not a good brain, and he's only twenty, and going through time slips over and over would probably scramble anyone's mind. So, fair enough.

The biggest villain seems to be Kayleigh's dad. If she'd lived in that neighbourhood with her mother instead of him, maybe all four of them could have grown up fine — but that isn't something you can change.. It's the best ending, but a painful one.

I also liked the scene where he talks to Kayleigh in one of the presents. However hard he tries on his own, however sincerely he speaks, he has still rewritten the past on his own terms — and to Kayleigh, who took that butterfly effect straight to the face, none of it lands as moving or meaningful at all.

---

There must be so many people who regret their past. Doesn't almost everyone have some small "it would have been better if I'd done it this way"? But everything has two sides. Me taking a chance means someone else missed it, and me missing one means someone else took it.

On top of that, people grow through what happens and what they see and hear every minute and every hour — so skipping that time and letting a mature me stand in for an immature me is also a failure to respect the not-yet-mature me right after the choice, and everyone around them. Each version of me had their own pace and situation, and chose accordingly.

Anyway, a film is only a film.. you can't actually rewind time. When something seems to be going wrong, the right move is to think about how to clean it up and how to move forward, not to regret.
