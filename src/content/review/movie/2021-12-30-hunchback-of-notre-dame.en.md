Spoilers ahead

It's the musical animation of The Hunchback of Notre Dame. I'd known it existed for ages but it looked boring so I never bothered — and coming to it late may actually have served me better.

## Plot
It's set in a past Paris where Roma people are persecuted. Quasimodo's mother is Roma; fleeing the judge, she is killed by him, and the Quasimodo she was carrying is raised locked in the bell tower as the judge's act of penance. Then, wanting to enjoy the festival day, he escapes, gets tormented by the crowd for his appearance, and is rescued by Esmeralda, which ties them together.

At that festival the judge sees Esmeralda and falls for her at first sight — he hates her for being Roma but loves her for being beautiful, and says she has bewitched him;;;
Quasimodo helps Esmeralda escape him. He comes to love her too, but she falls for the handsome, righteous Phoebus..

At the end Quasimodo saves Esmeralda from being burned at the stake, climbs the cathedral and shouts "Sanctuary!!" three times — a great scene.. In the fighting the judge falls to his death from a height, Esmeralda and Phoebus end up together, and Quasimodo comes out of the bell tower to live among the people he had longed for.

## Afterwards
First, the judge catching Roma people while singing that he is right, then trying to kill the infant Quasi as a monster before praying to God and deciding to raise him as penance, still insisting he is right.. Right before watching I'd seen an ad on Instagram for a book by the author Ppangttok, and a passage from it came back to me.

> [!quote]
> Everyone looks for the light. They seem to believe that if they find it, they'll have the answer to life. People who think they've found the light carry a certainty the rest of us don't. That certainty frightens me. People who believe they are unconditionally right push everyone else into the dark.

It's a line from the novel *Afraid of the Light*. That passage had already given me a lot to chew on, and the "people" in it look exactly like the judge. He is certain of his own actions, believes that certainty is the light, and slaughters Roma and citizens indiscriminately — and the staging increasingly frames him as someone better suited to hell.

But it wasn't only the judge; the townspeople were much the same. They tied Quasi up and jeered at him purely because of how he looks, and what people pray for in the cathedral is personal money, status, fame and blessings.

The shut-in Quasimodo brings a lot of Disney characters to mind. Rapunzel, Elsa, the Little Mermaid. Characters who want to connect with the world but are locked away. Unlike them, though, Quasimodo doesn't have the looks people like, and he doesn't get a romance.

The townspeople's brutality, the judge's mania, the failed unrequited love, the discrimination over appearance, the religion — those bleak elements are what set this apart from the others.

The crowd's attitude flipping at the very end and everyone being nice to Quasi is, well, unavoidable — a Disney animation is a fairy tale. Compared with the others, it feels like the side character next to the protagonist became the protagonist. In the end Quasi gets to blend in among people, while Esmeralda and Phoebus get the process and the ending of a typical Disney love story.

I really enjoyed it. It felt like Disney pouring everything into the bleak mood they'd occasionally reach for in the 19nn's. Not a film to watch when you want to feel good, but I got to sit with a religion-tinged, out-of-its-mind period story that you rarely get to see, without it stressing me out. Anyway, the atmosphere is incredible..
