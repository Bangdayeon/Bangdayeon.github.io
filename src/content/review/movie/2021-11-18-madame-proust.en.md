Spoilers ahead

The title, the poster and the cast all scream "French film", so even with everyone calling it a classic I kept putting it off. I'd dozed off partway through A Man Called Ove once, and worried this would go the same way...

But my god, it's so good.

## Plot
The protagonist's parents died, so he lives with his aunts. He can't remember his early childhood, but something in it shook him, and he hates his father and doesn't speak. Then he wanders into Madame Proust's flat by chance, eats a madeleine with tea, and starts seeing the infant memories he'd been living without.

Dad hit Mum → no, it was rehearsal for a show, it was a misunderstanding — he works that out, but something still doesn't sit right.

So he has more madeleines and tea and digs further, and learns that between the man who illegally remodelled the flat and the aunts hammering the piano upstairs, the piano fell through and crushed his parents. Hiding that and then making the child play *that* piano — is that not insane?

Meanwhile Madame Proust, who had been his comfort, dies of cancer while he isn't looking. He goes to leave his ukulele at her grave, but it starts raining and he takes it back so it won't be ruined.

Then, hearing the ukulele ring under the raindrops, he remembers Madame Proust and comes through his hard-luck life.

Years later he's married to the violin player, and they set off on a trip with their baby — and unlike the father who once terrified him in his pram and left an unconscious trauma, he smiles at his baby and says:

"I know that one day you'll remember this."

The warm mood, and the music running all through it, were lovely. A genuinely healing film!
