## Plot

Sang-goo (Lee Byung-hun), working as a gangster, is betrayed by a partner (the star columnist?) and loses his right hand.

A prosecutor (Cho Seung-woo) joins hands with Sang-goo to stop a presidential candidate who does every kind of bad thing behind a mask and wants the presidency. Their opponents are the ruling party's presidential candidate (polling 10%), a man who writes well, and the president of a car company — while Lee Byung-hun is a gangster who was betrayed and discarded by that big team, and Cho Seung-woo is a baby prosecutor with no connections who has only just moved over from the police.

The two of them take on the car company chairman, the 10% presidential candidate, and the writer whose words people believe.

## Afterwards

It was strange to finally see the famous scenes. The mojito-and-Maldives line, "I am the prosecution service of the Republic of Korea!", and the shot of Lee Byung-hun holding up his hand.. lol

I liked that the film ends satisfyingly. A baby prosecutor with no connections and a gangster beat three people with real clout.

I also like the title. I hadn't given a thought to why it's called Inside Men until Cho Seung-woo, working with Lee Byung-hun, walks out with the footage from the politicians' sex-buying party and the news anchor says "a man who describes himself as an insider~~" — and I went, ah, *that's* why it's Inside Men...!
