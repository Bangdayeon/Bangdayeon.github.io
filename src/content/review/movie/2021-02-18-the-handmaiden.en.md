I watched it ages ago, then it popped into my head out of nowhere, so this time I went with the extended cut.

They say Park Chan-wook likes pretty things, and you can really feel that he means it.

The house the lady lives in, the way the camera frames it, all the props — every bit of it is gorgeous.
This movie is a full-on feast for the eyes.. and the music is good too.

## Structure

The film splits cleanly into three acts. It flips over with a bang right in the middle.

- **Act 1** — Sook-hee, who is very good at stealing
- **Act 2** — Hideko's twist
- **Act 3** — Sook-hee + Hideko's escape

The story moves through those three points of view.

## Bits that stuck with me
In Park Chan-wook films there always seem to be moments where you feel like you shouldn't laugh but it's funny anyway. In The Handmaiden, Ha Jung-woo usually gets that job.

The first time around I was too busy going "wow, this is so pretty.." to take much else in, but the second time I found myself sitting much closer to Hideko's feelings.

There are little bits that got extended in this cut, and the one I remember is Hideko coming back from a reading and throwing up.

> [!quote]
> Hideko — It's fine.. I've just been reading too much..
>
> Sook-hee — Oh dear\~\~ just how hard have you been reading\~\~\~

So sad.. it must have been so hard and so disgusting.

## An interview I once saw
There's a shot early on where the housekeeper scurries down the hallway, and I have a vague memory of an interview where Park Chan-wook said he'd cut it for being too long and wished he'd left it in.. I can't remember whether it survived into the extended cut.

There's also a scene where Ha Jung-woo chomps through a peach, and I once saw an interview saying the peach was way juicier than expected, so Ha Jung-woo was flustered and Kim Tae-ri had a hard time keeping a straight face. I'd seen that before watching this cut, so this time I was laughing along with him during the peach scene.
