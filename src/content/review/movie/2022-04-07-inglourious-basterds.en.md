## Plot

It's a film about smashing Nazis. Unflinching action.

It opens with Nazi soldiers killing a Jewish family hidden in a farmhouse. The officer? Hans notices that the Jewish family doesn't understand English, and tells the farmer sheltering them to speak honestly in English right now or he'll kill the farmer's whole family — so the farmer says in English that Jews are hidden below..

Only one, Shosanna, survives and escapes, then runs a cinema in France under a false identity. A German war hero who single-handedly killed some three hundred men as a sniper sees her and falls for her.

Being a war hero he pulls some strings and arranges the premiere of the film about his own story at Shosanna's cinema. He presumably meant it as a way to impress her, but there's no way she could see this sniper kindly. Thanks to that, Shosanna makes a plan to kill the high-ranking officials who'll gather at her cinema. Meanwhile Hans, the capable head of security?, prepares against terrorism.

And the Basterds, an outfit famous for catching Nazis with uncanny skill, hear about the premiere separately from Shosanna and plan their own operation to wipe out the leadership. So both sides run toward the screening date.

## Plot with spoilers

The film flows so briskly and cheerfully that I assumed the Basterds wouldn't die, but almost all of them do. Shosanna dies, and so does the man running the projection.

The Basterds die fighting a Nazi group in a tavern, and Shosanna shoots the sniper who'd fallen for her, lets her guard down, and is shot by him.

But the leadership all die too. Hitler dies. Shot multiple times, I think.. Meanwhile Hans, predicting that the Nazis are finished once Hitler dies, phones the US government to say he'll play his part in killing Hitler if they'll wipe out his crimes as a Nazi, and the US government accepts.

So Hans walks out alive with one subordinate, runs into the two surviving Basterds in the woods, and gets a swastika carved into his forehead with a knife.

## Afterwards

It's rated for adults. Brutal scenes appear fairly unfiltered.

The strongest is the Basterds scalping a Nazi soldier early on; beyond that there's the shooting, the swastika carved into the forehead, and so on.

The actor playing Hans was extraordinary, and Hitler turning up now and then in a screaming rage was funny. Actually it isn't funny. It's staged to be easy to laugh at.

I was curious how a film about all the leadership including Hitler coming to a premiere and being killed there could possibly end, and the ending was clean and good.

Most Nazi-related films I've seen have been about the genocide of Jewish people. Films that make you register once more how horrific it was. Things like Life Is Beautiful, The Boy in the Striped Pyjamas, Schindler's List.

And then the war films, which show the horror of war well. Dunkirk, Saving Private Ryan.

But Basterds holds both of those relatively briefly and simply and mostly runs on a triumphant mood, being fiction — so I could watch it with a lighter heart and a sense of release. I liked that it was different in that way.
